### 2. Where and when (70 s) · `where`
NARRATION: An early dance named Cendrawasih was created by I Gde Manik in the 1920s within the Sawan subdistrict of Buleleng Regency, Bali—the same historic heartland that birthed classic pieces such as Trunajaya, Wirangjaya, and Palawakya.
NARRATION: However, that early 1920s iteration differed significantly from the modern repertory.
NARRATION: The current rendition of Cendrawasih stems from choreography designed by N. L. N. Swasthi Wijaya Bandem, premiered in 1988.

### 3. When it is danced (60 s) · `occasion`
NARRATION: Rooted in the natural world and Balinese cosmology, Cendrawasih draws upon the mythic status of the bird of paradise as manuk dewata—the celestial bird of the gods.
NARRATION: Outside of local cultural displays in Bali, the dance serves as a premier showcase piece for Indonesian cultural diplomacy abroad.
NARRATION: It has been featured at major international events, including performances in Peru in 2002, at the Freer Gallery of Art in Washington, D.C. in 2008, and across stage venues in Japan and the Netherlands.
NARRATION: Today it is a living tradition.
