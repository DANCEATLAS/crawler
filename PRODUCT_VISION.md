## 1. Mission, BDF and the present assignment

Build an exceptionally rigorous, culturally credible, multilingual environment in which people discover, understand, learn, create and share the world's dances. The central experience is **BDF — Best Dance Friend**: a personal AI dance mentor that responds to voice and movement and develops with the person. Through one familiar relationship, the learner reaches cultural knowledge, music discovery, excellent instruction, creative expression, physical activity and positive human connection.

The owner envisions a lifelong companion: from caregiver-led movement and music in infancy through childhood, adulthood and later life. “Zero to 200” expresses that lifelong ambition, not a lifespan prediction. The mentor adapts its language, tone, pace, explanations, interests and teaching to the individual and their chosen way of speaking. It can feel familiar and personal while remaining clearly an AI. It supports real relationships, community and independent agency; it must not encourage exclusivity, emotional dependence, secrecy from caregivers, or substitution for human care.

People learn alone, with a partner, in a global group or eventually among virtual dancers in their physical space. They explore history through movement, spirituality through respectful cultural understanding, musical traditions, celebrations and the meanings people give to dance. The owner wants a groundbreaking resource for physical and mental wellbeing. The ambition is the highest standard of dance teaching; demonstrate it through learning outcomes, cultural review and appropriate evaluation. It is not an established superiority, longevity or medical claim. Attractive animation, time spent and catalogue size do not prove learning or wellbeing.

**Now:** grow, correct and deepen content, reusable assets and scripts economically for years alongside the owner's other projects. Build the knowledge, teaching and permissions foundation needed by both traditional and Viral experiences. Keep progressing if a session, subscription or vendor disappears. **Later:** release only when the offered experience passes the gates below and the owner authorizes launch. Batch films/classes remain a delivery route; personalized BDF interaction is the preferred destination. No assumed launch date, automatic launch after a technology announcement or production spending is authorized here.

Do not assume the entire vision is impossible today or already solved. Evaluate components against Dance Atlas tasks and dated evidence. Keep the vision open to technology the owner cannot yet foresee, while preserving its purpose and standards. `TECHNOLOGY_READINESS.md` records assessments, not purchases or installed capabilities.

## 2. One mentor, three connected product areas

| Area | Purpose | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Atlas** | Deep profiles of traditions, established styles, regional forms, lineages and meaningful variants; endangered, rare, historical and revived dances | Preserve community identities. A song, person, festival or isolated move is not automatically a tradition |
| **Viral studio and culture** | Challenges, song choreographies, trends, K-pop challenges, dance memes, regional waves, flashmobs and historical crazes; learning, co-creation and distribution | First-class branch. A valid challenge or move can belong here without being an Atlas tradition |
| **Learn, create and dance together** | Personal teaching, partner practice, scheduled classes, friendships, collaborative recordings and performances, eventually AR | Uses reviewed knowledge/assets from both branches; capabilities depend on teaching, rights, safeguarding and technical readiness |

BDF is the conversational guide across these areas, not a fourth disconnected feature. A learner can discover a tradition, understand its music and history, practise it, find classmates, create a response or original choreography, and prepare a shared performance through the same mentor.

Use explicit relationships: derived from a tradition, popularized a move, uses a recording, choreographed by, adapted by, or related variant. Never collapse a challenge and an older tradition merely because they share a name. Never discard valid Viral entries under the Atlas's tradition-only audit rule. Non-dance memes may be contextual material, but cannot masquerade as teachable dances.

## 3. The learner's experience

### V01 — Welcoming, conversational discovery

Offer useful choices without requiring dance terminology: “Would you like something for yourself, for two people, or for a group? I can suggest a region, a tradition, a celebration, or a dance challenge.” Explain unfamiliar options in the chosen language. Allow skipped onboarding, direct browsing, revised preferences, text and touch alternatives.

Ask only what improves the experience: interests, goals, prior learning, available time and space, solo/partner/group preference, pace, language and optional accessibility needs. Explain optional camera/microphone uses before activation. Never silently infer religion, ethnicity, sex, health, disability or age from appearance, voice or movement.

### V02 — Meaningful, combinable discovery filters

| Dimension | Required distinctions |
| --- | --- |
| Place and community | Region, state/province, country, locality, community, diaspora and multiple origins; historical boundaries versus present borders |
| Tradition and belief | Traditional, sacred, ceremonial, social, theatrical and contemporary; religion/belief associations supported by sources |
| Purpose | Celebration, grief/mourning, courage, luck, courtship, weddings, harvest, seasonal/astronomical cycles, identity, storytelling and other sourced purposes |
| Preservation | Living, endangered, rare, revived or historical; assessor, evidence date and uncertainty. Low online visibility alone does not prove endangerment |
| Participation | Solo, two people, partnered, circle, line, ensemble or large formation; roles, contact and space requirements |
| Learning | Prerequisites, movement complexity, rhythm, intensity, practice time, available instruction, language and reviewed adaptations |
| Music and dress | Rhythmic framework, instruments, tempo when applicable, recordings, costume/outfit and ceremonial restrictions |
| Viral and creation | Category, track/version, creator, place, platform, era, popularity window, teachability, group size, content suitability and available creative assets |

Support multiple values and unknowns. Do not reduce transnational traditions to one country or equate historical practice with a rule every learner must follow. Distinguish historically gendered roles from current voluntary participation choices.

### V03 — Teaching responsive to voice and movement

The learner can ask BDF to demonstrate, repeat, slow down, explain a rhythm, change viewpoint, show left/right, practise a transition, answer a cultural question or change the lesson. The mentor selects reviewed units, adapts explanation and repetition, and keeps a consented learning record.

Movement feedback requires evaluated sensing. Start from optional device capabilities; unproven sensors or a headset cannot be mandatory for basic learning. Camera, microphone, wearables and spatial signals each need a purpose, permission and off switch. Prefer local processing where feasible; transmit and retain only what the chosen feature needs. Voice commands must work with music playing under tested conditions; pause/stop needs an accessible alternative.

Separate beat alignment, weight transfer, sequence, placement and expressive variation. A single similarity score is not a complete judgment. When the body is occluded, calibration fails or confidence is inadequate, state the limitation and offer a repeat or self-check. Do not confidently correct what cannot be observed or infer pain/weight bearing from an unsupported pose estimate.

Adaptation preserves essential technique and meaning. Seated, low-impact, space-limited and simplified versions are labeled adaptations with their own review state. Never invent missing mechanics to complete a class. Do not progress automatically into lifts, risky contact or demanding movements without validated prerequisites. Stopping and changing the activity must always be easy.

### V04 — BDF personality, language and continuity

Give each person a familiar mentor profile, with editable name/presentation and chosen speaking style, voice, language, energy, directness, detail and encouragement. Matching the user's style means understanding their preferences, not indiscriminately copying harmful language or pretending to be a human. A returning learner can continue from agreed goals and previous work without repeating everything.

Use a versioned, inspectable memory of preferences, learning progress and consent. Let the person correct, reset, export or delete it; keep inferred observations separate from confirmed facts. Do not create sensitive psychological profiles or retain raw camera/audio by default. Persist learning through vendor changes without locking identity to one model. Explain consequential changes to memory or personalization. Never use attachment to BDF as a spending or engagement pressure mechanism.

The earlier decision for one recognizable atlas narrator remains a default editorial identity. It does not prevent individually tailored BDF voices, tones and approved instructor presentations. Separate presentation from factual knowledge and motion so changing a personality cannot change the dance. Do not imitate a real teacher's likeness, voice or signature performance without documented permission.

Support discovery, cultural explanation, dialogue, captions, counting, pronunciation and feedback in the learner's language. Retain native terminology with transliteration/pronunciation support. Check equivalent movement meaning, timing and cues: literal translation and intact numbers are insufficient. Captions, text and non-voice controls remain available.

Costumes may be reconstructed from evidence with cultural review; neutral dress is the fallback where evidence or permission is insufficient. Never make sacred attire a decorative avatar option. Label reconstruction and distinguish traditional dress from a newly designed costume inspired by a style.

### V05 — Lifelong, age-appropriate participation

Infant/toddler experiences are **caregiver-led**: shared music, rhythm, play and appropriate movement with the adult in control, not an independent infant chatbot, open social account or autonomous movement coach. Do not claim that an infant can understand or consent to AI interaction. Qualified developmental review must shape any age-specific offering before release.

Childhood, adolescent, adult and later-life experiences require different language, instruction, privacy and participation designs. Include guardian controls where appropriate, transparent transitions as the child grows, and voluntary reassessment of goals. No covert tracking across life stages or assumption that early caregiver choices permanently define the person. Later-life adaptations must respect ability and preference rather than age stereotypes.

Do not launch children's social features until suitable age assurance, guardian oversight, moderation, reporting/blocking and adult/minor separation are reviewed and tested for the markets served. No unrestricted stranger contact with children. BDF can support wellbeing through movement and connection, but is not represented as a clinician or a replacement for care.

### V06 — Global classes and dance friendships

People can discover suitable classmates, choose individual/pair/group sessions, share an agreed classroom view, repeat classes and form continuing groups across countries. A session needs dance/variant, objective, level, language, host, format, capacity and a timezone-aware schedule shown locally. Support invitations, consented reminders, rescheduling and cancellation. Live, prerecorded and synchronized-playback modes are distinct.

Measure network timing; shared playback does not prove accurate real-time partner synchronization. When latency prevents reliable feedback, explain and use a suitable fallback. Users control camera, microphone, contact and recording access. Screen sharing is explicit. Joining a class is not consent to record, replay, publish or train on it. Each use needs its own permission; recording and public publication default off. Provide visible recording state, deletion and consent withdrawal with clear limits for already shared copies.

Age-appropriate groups, parental guidance, cultural preferences and voluntary gender/sex participation preferences belong to the design. Use explicit preferences and clear group policies, not demographic inference. Do not expose precise age or sensitive preferences publicly. Preserve inclusive participation where compatible with legitimate class context. Resolve relevant market requirements before launch, not through guessed legal claims in product copy.

### V07 — AR, stages and large formations

Future interfaces can place BDF or a teacher next to the learner, bring remote/virtual dancers into the room, create a stage and audience, or include the person in a giant dance arrangement. Preserve scale, floor contact, orientation, personal space and the difference between real and synthetic participants. Audience visibility, social presence and recording are controlled by the user.

Evaluate spatial calibration, latency, accessibility, device performance and comfort. Do not obscure hazards or encourage collisions. Offer screen-based equivalents where possible and an immediate pause/exit. Private practice, simulated audience and public performance are different modes. AR is a delivery layer over the same evidence and teaching library; no headset vendor owns the canonical content.

## 4. Viral: discovery, mentoring, creation and distribution

### V08 — Cultural context and creator credit

Each challenge/trend needs type, original or disputed creator, popularizers, original source, first documented date, context, exact music/version, variants/remixes, teaching status and suitability. Artist, songwriter, choreographer, performer and popularizer are separate roles. Preserve uncertainty and credible competing attribution instead of inventing one originator.

Explain the background, teach the documented sequence when permitted, and connect it to relevant traditions without suggesting one viral clip represents an entire culture. The learner can explore global profiles with BDF, discover a style that feels special, and understand what is appropriate to borrow, adapt or leave intact.

### V09 — Honest rankings and active refresh

Rankings state what is measured, supported platforms, geography if known, observation window, last successful update, missing coverage and method version. “This Month” means measured activity within a stated month/window, not cumulative views multiplied by the dance's origin date. “All Time” still has source-coverage limits. A newly catalogued dance is not necessarily newly popular.

Keep external views, estimated participation, velocity and in-app votes separate. One hero video's views are not total challenge participation. Do not fabricate platform-wide counts, treat missing values as zero popularity or combine incomparable metrics without explanation. Mitigate repeated votes/manipulation. Mark stale or insufficient data and show an honest dated fallback instead of false current ranks.

Use separate discovery, missing-media and refresh queues. Failed searches receive bounded retries/cooldowns; successful older entries are periodically rechecked. Balance volatile trends, historically significant crazes and underrepresented regions/languages within existing quotas. Respect source access terms. A permanent “already tried” flag cannot substitute for freshness.

### V10 — BDF as a creative partner

The complete creation journey is: discover or choose a challenge; understand its context; practise with BDF; receive supported tips; rehearse a chosen version; capture takes; assemble/edit; review credit, permissions and audience; export or share after confirmation. The learner can make their own response, collaborate with others or create a new original dance/challenge. Popularity is never guaranteed.

Support practice aids, breakdowns, cues, camera framing suggestions and reviewed technique tips. Creative assets may include permitted music, outfits, costumes, backgrounds, stages, captions, framing, transitions and approved visual effects. Keep decorative effects separate from instructional motion: editing must not make a physically impossible move look like correct technique. Clearly label synthetic alterations or generated performances when relevant.

Collaboration may use split screens, aligned clips, group mosaics, synchronized turns or shared performances. Track each participant's role, clip ownership, recording/publication permission and approved audience. Preserve collaborators' credits and let them review the assembled version before distribution. Do not create another person's likeness/performance without permission.

For a new dance derived from several traditions, record influences and distinguish original choreography from an authentic traditional form. Do not market a remix as a ritual, community-endorsed practice or historical reconstruction without evidence. Keep restricted movements, attire and contexts out of casual generation when permission is absent.

### V11 — Distribution, ownership and music

Prepare platform-appropriate exports: aspect ratio, captions, timing, credits, audio versions, quality and destination metadata. Authorized account connectors may later support publishing. Preparing a video, connecting an account and publishing publicly are separate actions. Require the user's explicit destination/audience approval for each publication or a clearly defined standing publishing rule. A discussion of this future feature does not authorize any current post.

Keep a distribution record linking the approved version, collaborators, music permissions and destinations. Support revisions/deletion where platforms permit and explain limits. Never promise complete retraction of copied public media. Avoid watermark/rights removal, impersonation, manipulative engagement or automatic spam across platforms. Let people enjoy private creation without pressure to publish.

The original song/edit can define a challenge. Record it even when reuse is unavailable. Reference links/embeds, licensed lesson audio, rights to publish a learner performance and an independently composed practice track are distinct uses. Label a substitute honestly; it is not the original soundtrack. Generating new music does not clear rights in an existing song, choreography, video or likeness.

## 5. Evidence and teaching assets built now

### V12 — Rigorous research and cultural care

Capture facts as claims with stable IDs, exact source locations/timestamps, evidence identity/date, quotation verification, support/contradiction assessment and review state. Separate observed facts, reconstruction, derived teaching, general presentation techniques and practitioner-approved instruction. Quote overlap proves only that text appears in a source; it does not establish every sentence's truth or support.

Use community/practitioner voices, scholarly work, archives and primary records. Compare credible conflicting sources; source scarcity is not permission to fill gaps. Trace aliases across languages/scripts, preserve meaningful variants and record reversible merge/retirement decisions with redirects. Popularity must not determine all research attention. Endangerment needs an attributed assessment and date, not an inference from few search results.

Public recordings do not automatically grant cultural permission to teach, adapt, reproduce attire or perform a ritual. Keep current sacred/restricted teaching holds unless appropriate authority and evidence support a reviewed change. Publicly shareable cultural context is distinct from instructional permission. History, spirituality, purpose, dress, music and movement must be described with precision and respect.

### V13 — A durable, reusable asset and learning-unit contract

| Layer | Required information for the relevant approved use |
| --- | --- |
| Identity | Stable tradition, variant, community, challenge and creator IDs; typed relationships and historical scope |
| Evidence | Claim IDs, source locations, support state, disagreements, review date, reviewer role and version |
| Movement | Starting posture, weight transfer, foot/body actions, direction, timing, rhythm, transitions, formation, partner roles and permitted reference motion |
| Teaching | Objective, prerequisites, demonstration, cues, slow practice, repetitions, common errors/evidenced corrections, progression check and limits |
| Adaptation | Allowed pace/view/explanation/difficulty changes, invariant technique, reviewed alternatives and unsupported requests |
| Music | Appropriate rhythmic framework, cycles, accents, tempo, microtiming, instruments, phrasing, form, tuning, movement relation and verified references |
| Language | Stable unit IDs, native terminology, pronunciation, localized instructions, equivalent meaning and native-review status |
| Creative production | Reviewed motion clips, permitted music/stems, wardrobe/background/stage assets, captions, framing templates, synchronized edits and synthetic-content labels |
| Permissions | Creator/provider, licence/terms/date, permitted uses, cultural restrictions, attribution, collaborator consent, expiry and review |
| Personal continuity | User-approved preferences/progress, memory provenance and controls; stored privately, separately from this public repository |

Do not force every dance into Western eight-counts, three arbitrary levels, one country or one teacher's motion. The current schema 5 profiles and episode packs are a starting point, not this complete implemented contract. Validate new fields against contrasting examples before migrating the full library.

Generate durable knowledge, composition/visual briefs, teaching units and reusable metadata now. Generate expensive production assets only after their acceptance process and budget are approved. Keep approved assets, source evidence, review records and change dependencies so future technology can improve delivery without repeating the research.

### V14 — Music worthy of the tradition

“Rights-free” means the goal of original music reusable commercially for intended product uses without recurring royalties under documented terms. Do not label all AI output copyright-free. Provider permission, ownership, third-party claims and cultural legitimacy are separate. Track composition, recording, performance, samples and model/input permissions as applicable. Public references are not automatically reusable production inputs.

Brief musicians/tools precisely: rhythm, swing/microtiming, instrument roles, tuning, phrasing, cues, form, tempo behavior, emotional/ceremonial context and movement relationship. Avoid generic “world music.” Full-tempo, slow-practice, cue and loop versions must still support correct learning. Keep permitted stems and synchronization metadata.

Tradition-specific musicians/practitioners review credibility and lesson fit before release. Automated scores cannot establish authenticity. Preserve human creative contributions, provenance, versions and acceptance records. Viral's original-track requirement remains separate from original-music production.

## 7. Release gates

Each gate applies to the experience offered; a failure cannot be compensated by a high average score.

1. **Evidence/culture:** supported claims, resolved material contradictions, correct identity/variants, restrictions honored and tradition-specific review.
2. **Teaching:** qualified review of mechanics, rhythm, progression, corrections and adaptations; representative learners achieve and retain predefined objectives.
3. **Motion/interaction:** reliable contact/weight transfer, orientation, timing, repeatability and sensing confidence; uncertainty and tracking loss handled honestly.
4. **Language/access:** native review, equivalent instructions, usable captions/controls and tested adaptations for the offered scope.
5. **Music/assets:** credible fit, documented permissions, attribution and appropriate synthetic-content labels.
6. **BDF/life stages:** transparent AI identity, controllable memory, appropriate age-specific design, caregiver control where needed and no deceptive dependence mechanisms.
7. **Social/creation/distribution:** consent, guardian protections where applicable, moderation, recording controls, collaborator review and explicit publication authority.
8. **Spatial delivery:** calibration, device, space and latency checks with fallback/exit for the offered AR mode.
9. **Operations/economics:** reproducibility, monitoring, recovery, rollback and measured total cost within an approved model.
10. **Owner decision:** explicit launch authorization. A demonstration website is not permission to launch or promote.

Prove the method on a small contrasting set while the atlas grows: different rhythms, cultures, solo/partner/group forms, languages and accessibility needs. Include demanding cases, not just vendor showcase examples. Avoid bulk production before acceptance works.
