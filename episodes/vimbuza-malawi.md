### 2. Where and when (70 s) · `where`
NARRATION: Vimbuza emerged among the Tumbuka of northern Malawi, eastern Zambia, and southern Tanzania during the nineteenth century, shaped by the Ngoni migrations known as the Mfecane and the convergence of Bemba, Bisa, Chewa, and Ngoni populations.
NARRATION: It developed partly as a replacement for mwavi, the poison ordeal suppressed by colonial authorities.
NARRATION: The Livingstonia Mission and colonial administrators instituted a formal ban around 1924 following a meeting at Elangeni in January 1920.

### 3. When it is danced (60 s) · `occasion`
NARRATION: Vimbuza is performed when an individual is diagnosed with spirit possession causing mental or physical illness, understood as an imbalance of hot and cold forces.
NARRATION: Predominantly involving women, the ritual functions as both medical treatment and a socially accepted release within patrilineal and virilocal marriage systems.
NARRATION: A secondary, profane form featuring satirical songs (vigerembo or vigetyo) also developed to air social criticisms.
