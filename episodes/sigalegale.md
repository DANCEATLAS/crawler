NARRATION: The Sigalegale puppet dance originates from the Batak Toba people of Samosir Island, Northern Sumatra, Indonesia.
NARRATION: It is linked to a local legend about Nai Manggale, a childless woman who instructed her husband to create a lifesize puppet of herself to prevent her spirit from cursing him after death.
NARRATION: Early 20th-century missionary Johannes Warneck described the use of the puppet in funerary ceremonies for wealthy men who died without heirs.

NARRATION: The Sigalegale dance is performed during papurpur sapata funerary ceremonies for persons of high rank who died without offspring.
NARRATION: It serves to dispel the curse of dying childless and to placate the spirit of the deceased.
NARRATION: The puppet acts as a symbolic child to perform funerary rites, ensuring the deceased's spirit is honored and does no harm to the community.
