### 2. Where and when (70 s) · `where`
NARRATION: Hula ʻauana developed in the 19th and 20th centuries as Hawaiian culture adapted to Western influences, specifically Christian morality and melodic harmony.
NARRATION: While hula was once banned or suppressed following the American overthrow of the Hawaiian Kingdom and the 1896 ban on the Hawaiian language in schools, a cultural resurgence in the late 1960s revitalized the practice.

### 3. When it is danced (60 s) · `occasion`
NARRATION: Hula ʻauana is performed to comment on significant people, places, or events, or to express emotions.
NARRATION: While it can be performed for entertainment at festivals like the Merrie Monarch (established 1971), it remains a vital method of preserving the Hawaiian language and oral history.
NARRATION: The concept of 'mana' (spiritual power) remains central to the poetry (mele), and performers are taught that they bear the consequences of the poetry they deliver.
