# THE ULTIMATE TEACHER — how the best online dance teachers teach

*Built by the machine from 18 tutorial lessons across 11 dances and 6 dance families (studied: 20 videos; 1 were not lessons; 1 could not be read). Updated 2026-11-05. It grows every day. The chapter scripts in `episodes/` follow it. See `MOVIE_PHASE.md`.*

## The lesson skeleton the best teachers share

1. **welcome and what you will learn**
2. **where the dance comes from**
3. **the whole step at full speed, once**
4. **breakdown, one movement at a time**
5. **slow practice**
6. **common mistakes and corrections**
7. **practice with the music, full tempo**
8. **adding the next layer (arms, turns, partner)**

## How they count

Most common: **5-6-7-8** — also: 1-2-3-4 (2); 1-3-5-7 with and-counts (and-1, and-3, and-5, and-7) (1); 1-and-3-and-5-and-7 (1); 1-2-3-4-5-6 (1)

## How they demonstrate

- views used by most teachers: front, back, mirror
- tempo progression: slow → half → full
- repetitions per step: about 4

## The cues they repeat

- and one and three and five and seven
- keep your feet together
- relax your knees
- and out and out and in and in
- double double
- and cross and cross
- right slap left slap
- bend the knees
- change the weight
- turn on the heels
- keep it small
- punch to the side

## Images they use

- the pinnacle of any classical ballet
- climbing Everest
- appetizer
- imagine you are like a doll and it's a plastic doll
- It's like riding a horse
- protecting your knees and your back so you're in a spring
- going to the toilet
- kicking this leg to the front

## Mistakes they name most

- doing squats instead of small forward and back movements
- making big upper body movements
- bringing upper body too much back
- bending both ankles
- turning on the toes instead of the heels
- putting the leg too far behind
- <not moving your head with the arm movement>
- don't tense your legs
- bringing ankles too close incorrectly
- arm too far behind yourself
- arm too behind follower
- too late with the under arm turn

## Body and safety advice

- relax your knees
- stay on your knees
- your body position needs to be here protecting your knees and your back so you're in a spring
- keep the angle between your hip and your upper body
- if your body starts to hurt, especially your lower back, stop
- try not to ever have two heels on the ground at a time
- take support like a wall or chair if you need confidence for the hop back
- people can roll their ankles if they get uncomfortable

## What makes a lesson absorbable

- clear visual examples from professional ballet performances
- structured timeline breaking down the grand pas de deux sections
- clear mirror angle showing both front and back view in reflection
- detailed breakdown of upper and lower body coordination
- clear progressive tempo buildup from slow to fast
- showing both front and back views for learning
- the teachers break down the steps into small, manageable parts
- they provide clear verbal and visual cues
- <detailed descriptions of arm and head coordination>
- back-facing angle makes mirroring natural

## What gets in the way

- none noted
- lots of historical and theoretical talk before any physical demonstration of sections
- long verbal explanations before full speed practice
- no mirrors or multiple students to follow along with
- the teachers do not provide a slow-motion demonstration of the full step
- <some camera angles are distant>
- no spoken audio explanation or music during tutorial
- rapid pace moving through many steps

## By dance family

**folk-circle** (4 lessons): counting 5-6-7-8; order: welcome and what you will learn → warm-up → where the dance comes from → the count, out loud → breakdown, one movement at a time → slow practice → follow-along with the teacher → practice with the music, full tempo; cues: and out and out and in and in, double double, and cross and cross, right slap left slap, side side side side
**street-urban** (3 lessons): counting 1-2-3-4-5-6; order: welcome and what you will learn → the whole step at full speed, once → breakdown, one movement at a time → practice with the music, full tempo → where the dance comes from → adding the next layer (arms, turns, partner); cues: punch to the side, pull your head down, pivot them on your elbows, lift your foot, then kick your leg
**latin-caribbean** (3 lessons): counting 1-3-5-7 with and-counts (and-1, and-3, and-5, and-7); order: welcome and what you will learn → where the dance comes from → the whole step at full speed, once → breakdown, one movement at a time → slow practice → adding the next layer (arms, turns, partner) → practice with the music, full tempo; cues: and one and three and five and seven, keep your feet together, relax your knees, bend the knees, change the weight
**ballroom-partner** (3 lessons): counting 4-1-2-3-4; order: welcome and what you will learn → where the dance comes from → the whole step at full speed, once → breakdown, one movement at a time → common mistakes and corrections; cues: side replace close, rock replace side, side with the left, bring it in, switch weight
**african-diaspora** (3 lessons): counting 5-6-7-8 and 1-2-3-4-5-6-7; order: welcome and what you will learn → where the dance comes from → breakdown, one movement at a time → adding the next layer (arms, turns, partner) → the whole step at full speed, once; cues: left foot, right foot, step, touch, stomp off
**classical-court** (2 lessons): counting 2 counts off, 2 counts on; order: welcome and what you will learn → where the dance comes from → breakdown, one movement at a time → common mistakes and corrections → practice with the music, full tempo; cues: hold your placement, stay straight as a board, lift up in your lower abs, change feet

## Method

Each lesson is watched once by a vision-language model (first eight minutes, public YouTube URL, nothing downloaded) and reduced to a fixed form: structure with timestamps, counting, cues, demonstration, mistakes, safety, steps with counts, absorbability, verbatim quotes. The lists above are frequencies across all lessons; a line appears only when at least one teacher did it. Per-video records: `pedagogy/videos/`. Aggregate: `pedagogy/teaching_patterns.json`.
