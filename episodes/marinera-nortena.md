### 7. The people (60 s) · `people`
NARRATION: Abelardo Gamarra Rondó ('El Tunante') named the dance in 1879 and penned early lyrics; José Alvarado ('Alvaradito') composed early melodies including 'La Concheperla' in 1892; Rosa Mercedes Ayarza de Morales transcribed 'La Concheperla' into sheet music notation; and singer Augusto Áscuez Villanueva (1892–1985) remains one of its foundational interpreters.
NARRATION: The northern coastal variant (Marinera Norteña) is faster, more flirtatious, and requires barefoot female dancers, distinguishing it from the slower, highly formal Marinera Limeña of the capital and the sentimental, minor-key Marinera Serrana of the Andean highlands.
NARRATION: Marinera Norteña evolved from the colonial zamacueca and related 19th-century styles such as the chilena and tondero, blending Spanish salon dances with native coastal and Afro-Peruvian rhythm traditions.
