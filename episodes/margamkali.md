NARRATION: Margamkali is an ancient round dance of the Saint Thomas Christian community in Kerala, primarily maintained by the endogamous Knanaya (Southist) sub-ethnic group.
NARRATION: Its origins are linked by scholars to Malabar Jewish diaspora dance patterns as well as Kerala Brahmin performance arts such as Sangam Kali and Yathra Kali.
NARRATION: The word 'Margam' signifies the path to salvation, and historically 'Margam Koodal' referred to Christian conversion.

### 4. Watch it (75 s) · `watch`
NARRATION: The dance is performed in a circular formation around a lit brass lamp (Nilavilakku).
NARRATION: Typically executed by a dozen dancers, the performance consists of two main parts (padham): a narrative singing and clapping phase recounting Saint Thomas's life, followed by Parichamuttukali, a martial phase incorporating artificial swords and shields.
NARRATION: Dancers synchronize rhythmic stepping and hand-clapping to the timekeeping of palm-sized cymbals played by the singer.
