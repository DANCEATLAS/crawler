# THE ULTIMATE TEACHER — how the best online dance teachers teach

*Built by the machine from 53 tutorial lessons across 34 dances and 7 dance families (studied: 62 videos; 2 were not lessons; 7 could not be read). Updated 2026-11-12. It grows every day. The chapter scripts in `episodes/` follow it. See `MOVIE_PHASE.md`.*

## The lesson skeleton the best teachers share

1. **welcome and what you will learn**
2. **where the dance comes from**
3. **the whole step at full speed, once**
4. **warm-up**
5. **breakdown, one movement at a time**
6. **slow practice**
7. **practice with the music, full tempo**
8. **recap and homework**

## How they count

Most common: **5-6-7-8** — also: 1-2-3-4 (5); 1-2-3 / 1-2-3-4-5-6-7-8 (1); 1-2 / 7-and (1); none (1)

## How they demonstrate

- views used by most teachers: front, back, side
- tempo progression: slow → half → full
- repetitions per step: about 4

## The cues they repeat

- side close side
- rock step
- out in out half circle back close
- one two tap accent with left shoulder
- one and two and three
- contract the pelvis it will pull you forward
- right leg out front
- jump split your legs
- the back foot goes up
- lift the left leg up
- keep your feet pointed
- use your core

## Images they use

- 180 degrees, not a complete circle
- pulling up the hip, letting it fall down
- the pinnacle of any classical ballet
- climbing Everest
- appetizer
- The feeling is like unhook and immediately hook again
- imagine you are like a doll and it's a plastic doll
- It's like riding a horse

## Mistakes they name most

- not using weight transfer
- changing height during the sit movement
- not pointing feet
- not engaging core and glutes
- doing squats instead of small forward and back movements
- making big upper body movements
- bringing upper body too much back
- bending both ankles
- turning on the toes instead of the heels
- putting the leg too far behind
- hoping instead of shifting weight
- leading step size from the follower instead of the leader

## Body and safety advice

- none
- prepare safety mats
- aerial is a dangerous activity
- do not attempt alone without consulting professionals
- make sure there is a good space between your head and the ground
- relax your knees
- stay on your knees
- your body position needs to be here protecting your knees and your back so you're in a spring

## What makes a lesson absorbable

- demonstration from multiple angles
- clear step-by-step breakdown
- clear verbal instructions
- clear verbal cues
- repetition of steps
- clear visual examples from professional ballet performances
- structured timeline breaking down the grand pas de deux sections
- side angle view for clear visibility of footwork
- counting aloud with the movements
- gradual addition of style and speed

## What gets in the way

- none noted
- fast-paced transitions between steps
- lots of historical and theoretical talk before any physical demonstration of sections
- no counting scheme
- long verbal explanations before full speed practice
- no music during the initial breakdown
- no mirrors or multiple students to follow along with
- the lack of verbal cues for the upper body movement

## By dance family

**folk-circle** (14 lessons): counting 5-6-7-8; order: welcome and what you will learn → the count, out loud → the whole step at full speed, once → warm-up → breakdown, one movement at a time → slow practice → practice with the music, full tempo → recap and homework; cues: rock step, side close side, triple step, rock step triple step triple step, step your right foot sideward
**street-urban** (13 lessons): counting 5-6-7-8; order: welcome and what you will learn → the whole step at full speed, once → the count, out loud → where the dance comes from → breakdown, one movement at a time → adding the next layer (arms, turns, partner) → slow practice → practice with the music, full tempo; cues: right leg out front, jump split your legs, the back foot goes up, lift the left leg up, jump back on the left leg
**african-diaspora** (10 lessons): counting 5-6-7-8; order: welcome and what you will learn → where the dance comes from → the whole step at full speed, once → breakdown, one movement at a time → slow practice → practice with the music, full tempo → adding the next layer (arms, turns, partner); cues: out in out half circle back close, one two tap accent with left shoulder, one and two and three, contract the pelvis it will pull you forward, and one and three and five and seven
**stage-contemporary** (5 lessons): counting none; order: welcome and what you will learn → warm-up → slow practice → breakdown, one movement at a time → the whole step at full speed, once; cues: keep your feet pointed, use your core, lean your body back, make a little bit space between feet, try to find some groove
**classical-court** (4 lessons): counting 5-6-7-8; order: welcome and what you will learn → warm-up → where the dance comes from → breakdown, one movement at a time → common mistakes and corrections → practice with the music, full tempo; cues: keep length in both sides of the waist, keep the supporting leg strong and stable, keep the spine long, demi-plie, pas de bourree
**ballroom-partner** (4 lessons): counting 1-2-3-4; order: welcome and what you will learn → where the dance comes from → the whole step at full speed, once → breakdown, one movement at a time → common mistakes and corrections; cues: side close side, step and point, turn turn turn, replace, side replace close
**ritual-sacred** (2 lessons): counting 1-2-3-4-5; order: welcome and what you will learn → the whole step at full speed, once → where the dance comes from → breakdown, one movement at a time → practice with the music, full tempo → recap and homework → slow practice; cues: hold your knife out, every time the blade faces down, two thumbs are facing up, one two, over and spin

## Method

Each lesson is watched once by a vision-language model (first eight minutes, public YouTube URL, nothing downloaded) and reduced to a fixed form: structure with timestamps, counting, cues, demonstration, mistakes, safety, steps with counts, absorbability, verbatim quotes. The lists above are frequencies across all lessons; a line appears only when at least one teacher did it. Per-video records: `pedagogy/videos/`. Aggregate: `pedagogy/teaching_patterns.json`.
