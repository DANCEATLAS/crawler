## What changes in the requirements investigation

1. **Retained learning needs a declared support condition.** Existing retention language lacks a concrete test that distinguishes coach-dependent performance from retained skill. Propose recording the unit, instructional prompts, essential music/calls, access supports, delay, practice between tests and independent scoring. Keep necessary access supports. Compare schedules rather than assuming less feedback is better.

2. **Feedback must use the reviewed adaptation.** A visually different performance may satisfy the agreed movement purpose. Propose explicit intent/invariants, allowed differences and unobservable dimensions, bound to the selected version and correction rubric. This is more precise than merely adding a seated option. Applicability and cultural permission need human review.

3. **Dance sensing and command gestures need separate controls.** Disabling gesture commands should not automatically disable permitted movement feedback. Add accidental-command replay tests and conventional/assistive control checks. Recording, invitations and publishing still need their separate deliberate permissions.

These are candidate methods and requirements; no Dance Atlas learning improvement, control reliability or adaptation validity was demonstrated.

## Evidence inspected

### S1 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7644831/

Published: 1995-06; inspected 2026-11-12. Location: Abstract, sentences 1–7; DOI 10.1080/02701367.1995.10762218.

In a rhythmical dance synchronization experiment, an auditory model improved acquisition synchronization scores but the advantage did not persist at retention. Authors suggest information dependency.

Abstract inspected; full methods, sample size and retention interval not verified. Does not establish an optimal feedback schedule for Dance Atlas.

### S2 — https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.02371/full

Published: 2018-11-30; inspected 2026-11-12. Location: Methods: Participants; Retention test; Evaluation and analysis of video material; Abstract.

Eighteen second-year dance students learned two phrases; thirteen returned for testing 10–13 days later without reproduction assistance, after brief practice. Evaluators used concealed condition identifiers. Visual-first learning improved recall/model approximation; interpretation ratings did not differ by initial modality.

Young trained students with normal/corrected vision, not an accessibility or beginner validation. The experiment does not justify imposing visual-first instruction on every learner.

### S3 — https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/motion-actuation.html

Published: not verified; inspected 2026-11-12. Location: SC 2.5.4; Intent paragraphs 1–5; essential-motion exception.

W3C explains alternative controls and disabling motion actuation for camera/device gestures, with exceptions including motion essential to the function.

Informative explanation of a standard, not a Dance Atlas usability test or a finding of legal compliance. No revision date verified.

### S4 — https://diff.wikimedia.org/2024/11/04/reviving-tuluvas-aati-month-saving-the-endangered-mugera-aati-kalenja-tradition/

A documentation participant describes community-led revival of the Mugera ritual, spirit impersonation, household visits and transmission to younger community members.

Evidence of this local revival, not permission for outsiders to teach, adapt or impersonate; not a 2026 prevalence census. Already a profile source, revisited for applicability.

Published: not verified; inspected 2026-11-12. Location: Translation, paragraphs 1–3.

Stopgap describes jointly translating movement across different physicalities by examining effort, spine, focus, direction, rhythm and detail, instead of assuming imitation of conventional steps.

First-party practitioner method, not a controlled efficacy trial or permission to adapt another community’s ritual. No publication date verified.
