## What the research says

Each finding stands on words copied from the source and checked against it. Most studies are about learning movement in general, not dance.

### Autonomy support
Supporting a learner's autonomy through small choices improves skill acquisition and retention, even when those choices are entirely unrelated to the physical task.
- Providing learners with a minor choice regarding task conditions improves their performance accuracy on later retention tests. — "Autonomy support and enhanced expectancies had additive advantages for learning, with both main effects being significant for retention and transfer." ([source](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/25046402))
- Self-controlled practice choices can enhance learning outcomes without needing to provide task-relevant information or strategic advantages. — "Thus, self-controlled practice conditions can influence motor learning without providing task-relevant information, content, or strategic learning advantages." ([source](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/25732095))
- Offering choices about matters completely unrelated to the core movement task can still result in more effective learning on retention tests. — "Balance learning was significantly more effective in the choice group on a retention test." ([source](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/25732095))
- Combining autonomy support with enhanced expectancies and an external focus of attention optimizes overall learning outcomes. — "On a delayed retention test, the AS-EE-EF group outperformed all other groups." ([source](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/28056637))
- For the teacher: Allow learners to make small choices during the lesson, such as selecting the color of a virtual prop or the order of practice routines.
- For the teacher: Combine autonomy support with positive feedback regarding the learner's progress to further enhance motor learning.

### Early success
Enhancing learner expectancies for successful performance and providing early positive experiences help build motivation and support motor learning.
- Conditions that enhance expectancies for future performance contribute to motor performance and learning by strengthening goal-action coupling. — "These include research on (a) conditions that enhance expectancies for future performance" ([source](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/26833314))
- Expected success helps consolidate memories and propels the cognitive and motor systems forward. — "Expected success presumably breeds further success and helps consolidate memories." ([source](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/26833314))
- Groups experiencing higher success rates and enhanced expectancies can make more hits and achieve heightened outcome expectancies. — "As predicted, the Large-target group made more hits and had heightened outcome expectancies compared to the Small-target group." ([source](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/31126044))
- For the teacher: Design early exercises to ensure learners achieve immediate successful outcomes to boost confidence and expectancy.

### Enjoyment and staying with it
Exercise enjoyment, which heavily drives continued participation, is strongly boosted by music satisfaction, positive instructor interactions, and supportive social or partner dynamics.
- Satisfaction with the music used in an exercise setting accounts for the largest share of variance in exercise enjoyment. — "satisfaction with music (21%) accounted for the most variance in exercise enjoyment" ([source](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/17590968))
- Participants experience greater enjoyment and achievement when dancing with partners who share good rapport and compatible approaches. — "gained greater enjoyment and sense of achievement from dance classes in comparison to couples who did not enjoy dancing together" ([source](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/28482703))
- Enjoyment is a significant predictor of out-of-class physical activity levels among participants. — "Significant effects of gender, group, enjoyment, and intensity on out-of-class weekly aerobic activity were observed" ([source](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/27723781))
- For the teacher: Play music that learners genuinely enjoy to maximize their overall satisfaction and motivation.
- For the teacher: Foster positive social interactions and community elements within the online platform to support engagement and well-being.

### Expectancies and autonomy
Enhanced performance expectancies and autonomy support both improve motor learning and performance, and their benefits can be additive when combined.
- Expectancy effects may be rooted in dopamine responses to anticipated positive experiences during practice. — "We describe a plausible mechanism for expectancy effects rooted in responses of dopamine to the anticipation of positive experience and temporally associated with skill practice." ([source](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/26833314))
- Providing autonomy support and enhanced expectancies independently and jointly benefit motor skill learning. — "Thus, supporting learners' need for autonomy by given them a small choice--even though it was not directly related to task performance--and enhancing their performance expectancies appeared to independently influence learning." ([source](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/25046402))
- The combination of autonomy support and enhanced expectancies yields the greatest accuracy during retention and transfer tests. — "Autonomy support and enhanced expectancies had additive advantages for learning, with both main effects being significant for retention and transfer. On both tests, the AS/EE group showed the greatest throwing accuracy." ([source](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/25046402))
- Positive affect is heightened by enhanced expectancies and predicts better transfer performance. — "Positive affect was heightened in the enhanced expectancy and enhanced expectancy groups after practice and predicted transfer performance." ([source](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/24875153))
- For the teacher: Provide learners with small choices during practice, such as selecting the color of a practice tool, to support their autonomy.
- For the teacher: Give positive social-comparative feedback to enhance learners' performance expectancies.
- For the teacher: Combine autonomy support and enhanced expectancies in lessons to maximize learning and retention accuracy.

### External focus
Directing a learner's attention to the external effects of their movements rather than to the body movements themselves enhances motor learning and performance.
- An external focus of attention on the intended movement effect helps establish efficient functional connections across brain networks that support skilled movement. — "Furthermore, we consider the influence of an external focus for the establishment of efficient functional connections across brain networks that subserve skilled movement." ([source](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/26833314))
- Increasing the physical distance between the body and the external effect of the action can further enhance learning advantages. — "The purpose of the present study was to test the hypothesis that increasing the distance between the body and the action effects might further enhance the learning advantages associated with an external focus of attention." ([source](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/12589447))
- Instructions or feedback that induce an external focus of attention are generally more effective than those prompting an internal focus. — "instructions or feedback inducing an external focus may be more effective than those with an internal focus." ([source](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/16777770))
- Providing feedback that emphasizes movement effects leads to greater task accuracy than feedback that focuses on body movements. — "External-focus feedback resulted in greater accuracy than internal-focus feedback did." ([source](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/12057890))
- For the teacher: Instruct users to focus on the external effects of their movements rather than on how their body parts are moving.
- For the teacher: Design feedback cues that direct attention further away from the user's body to promote more effective movement control.

### Self efficacy
Belief in one's ability to succeed, supported by choice and positive expectations, directly improves the learning of motor skills.
- Giving learners a small choice and positive performance expectations improves their accuracy and increases their self-efficacy. — "Autonomy support and enhanced expectancies had additive advantages for learning, with both main effects being significant for retention and transfer." ([source](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/25046402))
- External focus instructions combined with enhanced expectancies increase self-efficacy and improve motor skill retention and transfer. — "Furthermore, self-efficacy was increased by both external focus and enhanced expectancy, and predicted retention and transfer performance." ([source](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/24875153))
- Allowing learners to control when they view video feedback leads to greater increases in self-efficacy and better skill progression. — "Analysis of the acquisition data showed the LC group had greater increases in self-efficacy as compared to the EC group." ([source](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/23355826))
- For the teacher: Give learners small choices during practice, such as picking the practice speed or song variation, to support their sense of autonomy.
- For the teacher: Let learners choose when they want to view video recordings of their own movement.

### Educating students for a lifetime of physical activity: enhancing mindfulness, motivation, and meaning
To foster lifelong physical activity, teachers should create a nurturing environment that emphasizes personal meaning, cognitive decision-making, and self-motivation.
- Teachers should prioritize strategies that encourage students to engage in physical activity outside of the classroom setting. — "It will require teachers and program leaders to focus programmatic attention on strategies to actually increase students' out-of-class physical activity behavior." ([source](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/28742426))
- A supportive and motivating teaching environment is essential for creating transformative learning experiences. — "Transformative PE provides physical activity content within a nurturing and motivating environment that can change students' lives." ([source](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/28742426))
- Connecting physical activities to personal meaning helps students find relevance and motivation. — "It focuses on PE students' role in cognitive decision making, self-motivation, and their search for personal meaning that can add connection and relevance to physical activities." ([source](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/28742426))
- For the teacher: Ask students to reflect on what personal meaning they find in a specific dance style to increase their connection to the movement.
- For the teacher: Provide suggestions for how students can practice or explore the dance steps in their own time outside of the lesson.
- For the teacher: Use encouraging language that highlights the student's autonomy in making cognitive decisions about their own movement style.
