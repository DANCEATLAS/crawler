NARRATION: The Juba dance was brought by enslaved peoples from the Kongo to Charleston, South Carolina, as early as the 18th century.
NARRATION: It originated from the Kikongo word 'diuba,' meaning to beat the sun or time, and was originally a sacred step dance called giouba.
NARRATION: On American plantations, it became a vital means of preserving African identity when traditional rhythm instruments were banned by slaveholders who feared secret codes hidden in drumming.

### 7. The people (60 s) · `people`
NARRATION: The most famous Juba dancer was William Henry Lane, known as Master Juba, who was one of the first Black performers in the United States to achieve international fame.

NARRATION: The dance was practiced across the American South, the Caribbean (where it was known as Djouba in Haiti), and Dutch Guiana (Suriname).
