# THE ULTIMATE TEACHER — how the best online dance teachers teach

*Built by the machine from 58 tutorial lessons across 35 dances and 7 dance families (studied: 68 videos; 2 were not lessons; 8 could not be read). Updated 2026-11-13. It grows every day. The chapter scripts in `episodes/` follow it. See `MOVIE_PHASE.md`.*

## The lesson skeleton the best teachers share

1. **welcome and what you will learn**
2. **where the dance comes from**
3. **the whole step at full speed, once**
4. **warm-up**
5. **breakdown, one movement at a time**
6. **slow practice**
7. **practice with the music, full tempo**
8. **recap and homework**

## How they count

Most common: **5-6-7-8** — also: 1-2-3-4 (6); 1-2-3-4-5 (2); 12-1-2-3 / 12-1-2-3-4-5-6 (1); 5-6-7-8 and 1-and-2-and-3-and-4-and (1)

## How they demonstrate

- views used by most teachers: front, side, back
- tempo progression: slow → half → full
- repetitions per step: about 4

## The cues they repeat

- rock step
- side close side
- keep those abs pulled in
- engage all the muscles
- keep those shoulders down
- control control
- make sure you touch all the way back
- down back out back up back out back
- make sure that you are wearing comfortable shoes
- stand properly
- don't do that
- hold your knife out

## Images they use

- Pulling a bra strap in the front up
- Pulling a bra strap in the back down
- Dancing through syrup
- Goddess poses
- Indian statues
- The feeling is like unhook and immediately hook again
- Use your arms as a balancer
- the first swing uses force to create the swing, but then you need to relax

## Mistakes they name most

- lifting the shoulders while lifting the rib cage
- bending over while opening the rib cage
- moving shoulders or upper body instead of keeping it still
- boxing or trying to fight someone with arms
- not pointing feet
- not engaging core and glutes
- moving everything below the elbow instead of including the elbow
- not doing the arm movement around the head separately
- doing steps too big to speed up later
- making sounds behind your body like a wall
- not maintaining enough tension in the movements
- not coordinating the head and body movements

## Body and safety advice

- none
- if your body starts to hurt, especially your lower back, stop
- make sure that you have comfortable shoes before you start this stuff, especially if you're still trying to learn, because you don't want to incur some injuries
- prepare safety mats
- aerial is a dangerous activity
- do not attempt alone without consulting professionals
- make sure there is a good space between your head and the ground
- relax & breath

## What makes a lesson absorbable

- clear step-by-step breakdown
- clear verbal instructions
- demonstration from multiple angles
- clear, step-by-step breakdown of complex movements
- use of relatable metaphors to explain body mechanics
- breaking down complex sequences into one arm at a time before combining them
- repeating sides and going over the choreography multiple times
- breaking down the upper and lower body separation clearly
- showing multiple angles (front, side, back)
- clear counting on every blade rotation

## What gets in the way

- none noted
- the fast pace of the music practice might be challenging for some
- fast transition into full speed without intermediate tempo increments
- long conversational intros before getting into the movement
- rapid transitions between traditional and new styles without much pause
- no counting scheme
- only side profile is shown during breakdown
- the counting scheme is quite complex and might be difficult for beginners to follow

## By dance family

**street-urban** (16 lessons): counting 5-6-7-8; order: welcome and what you will learn → the whole step at full speed, once → where the dance comes from → breakdown, one movement at a time → slow practice → adding the next layer (arms, turns, partner) → practice with the music, full tempo → recap and homework; cues: make sure you touch all the way back, down back out back up back out back, feet parallel, relax  breath, swing stop
**folk-circle** (16 lessons): counting 1-2-3-4; order: welcome and what you will learn → the count, out loud → the whole step at full speed, once → warm-up → breakdown, one movement at a time → slow practice → practice with the music, full tempo → recap and homework; cues: keep those abs pulled in, engage all the muscles, keep those shoulders down, control control, left foot go
**african-diaspora** (10 lessons): counting 1-2-3-4; order: welcome and what you will learn → where the dance comes from → the whole step at full speed, once → breakdown, one movement at a time → slow practice → practice with the music, full tempo → adding the next layer (arms, turns, partner); cues: make sure that you are wearing comfortable shoes, stand properly, don't do that, and out and out and in and in, double double
**stage-contemporary** (5 lessons): counting none; order: welcome and what you will learn → warm-up → slow practice → breakdown, one movement at a time → the whole step at full speed, once; cues: keep your feet pointed, use your core, lean your body back, knees to chest, straightening my legs
**classical-court** (4 lessons): counting 5-6-7-8; order: welcome and what you will learn → warm-up → where the dance comes from → breakdown, one movement at a time → common mistakes and corrections → practice with the music, full tempo; cues: keep length in both sides of the waist, keep the supporting leg strong and stable, keep the spine long, demi-plie, pas de bourree
**ballroom-partner** (4 lessons): counting 4-1-2-3; order: welcome and what you will learn → where the dance comes from → the whole step at full speed, once → breakdown, one movement at a time → common mistakes and corrections; cues: step to side on 4-1, crossover with your left foot, transfer your weight fully, transfer your weight back, bring your leg back
**ritual-sacred** (2 lessons): counting 1-2-3-4-5; order: welcome and what you will learn → the whole step at full speed, once → where the dance comes from → breakdown, one movement at a time → practice with the music, full tempo → recap and homework → slow practice; cues: hold your knife out, every time the blade faces down, two thumbs are facing up, one two, over and spin

## Method

Each lesson is watched once by a vision-language model (first eight minutes, public YouTube URL, nothing downloaded) and reduced to a fixed form: structure with timestamps, counting, cues, demonstration, mistakes, safety, steps with counts, absorbability, verbatim quotes. The lists above are frequencies across all lessons; a line appears only when at least one teacher did it. Per-video records: `pedagogy/videos/`. Aggregate: `pedagogy/teaching_patterns.json`.
