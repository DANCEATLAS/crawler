# THE ULTIMATE TEACHER — how the best online dance teachers teach

*Built by the machine from 34 tutorial lessons across 26 dances and 8 dance families (studied: 38 videos; 2 were not lessons; 2 could not be read). Updated 2026-11-08. It grows every day. The chapter scripts in `episodes/` follow it. See `MOVIE_PHASE.md`.*

## The lesson skeleton the best teachers share

1. **welcome and what you will learn**
2. **warm-up**
3. **the whole step at full speed, once**
4. **where the dance comes from**
5. **breakdown, one movement at a time**
6. **slow practice**
7. **practice with the music, full tempo**

## How they count

Most common: **5-6-7-8** — also: 1-2-3-4 (5); 1-3-5-7 with and-counts (and-1, and-3, and-5, and-7) (1); rock-step / triple-step / triple-step (1); 1-and-3-and-5-and-7 (1)

## How they demonstrate

- views used by most teachers: front, back, side
- tempo progression: slow → half → full
- repetitions per step: about 4

## The cues they repeat

- side close side
- rock step
- and one and three and five and seven
- keep your feet together
- relax your knees
- step and point
- turn turn turn
- replace
- and out and out and in and in
- double double
- and cross and cross
- right slap left slap

## Mistakes they name most

- doing squats instead of small forward and back movements
- making big upper body movements
- bringing upper body too much back
- bending both ankles
- turning on the toes instead of the heels
- putting the leg too far behind
- hoping instead of shifting weight
- leading step size from the follower instead of the leader
- <not moving your head with the arm movement>
- moving everything below the elbow instead of including the elbow
- doing the lock really, really different and really weird
- don't tense your legs

## Body and safety advice

- relax your knees
- stay on your knees
- your body position needs to be here protecting your knees and your back so you're in a spring
- keep the angle between your hip and your upper body
- none
- make sure that you have comfortable shoes before you start this stuff, especially if you're still trying to learn, because you don't want to incur some injuries
- if your body starts to hurt, especially your lower back, stop
- try not to ever have two heels on the ground at a time

## What makes a lesson absorbable

- clear visual examples from professional ballet performances
- structured timeline breaking down the grand pas de deux sections
- clear mirror angle showing both front and back view in reflection
- detailed breakdown of upper and lower body coordination
- clear division into three parts
- constant verbal counting and foot clarification
- clear progressive tempo buildup from slow to fast
- showing both front and back views for learning
- clear breakdown of footwork for leaders and followers separately
- demonstration from multiple angles

## What gets in the way

- none noted
- lots of historical and theoretical talk before any physical demonstration of sections
- long verbal explanations before full speed practice
- no music during the initial breakdown
- no mirrors or multiple students to follow along with
- the teachers do not provide a slow-motion demonstration of the full step
- long explanations before dancing
- rapid transitions between traditional and new styles without much pause

## By dance family

**folk-circle** (9 lessons): counting 5-6-7-8; order: welcome and what you will learn → where the dance comes from → the count, out loud → breakdown, one movement at a time → practice with the music, full tempo → slow practice; cues: and out and out and in and in, double double, and cross and cross, right slap left slap, rock step
**street-urban** (7 lessons): counting 1-2-3-4-5-6; order: welcome and what you will learn → the whole step at full speed, once → breakdown, one movement at a time → where the dance comes from → practice with the music, full tempo → slow practice; cues: punch to the side, pull your head down, pivot them on your elbows, swing stop, lean forward
**african-diaspora** (5 lessons): counting 1-2-3-4; order: welcome and what you will learn → where the dance comes from → the whole step at full speed, once → breakdown, one movement at a time → practice with the music, full tempo → adding the next layer (arms, turns, partner); cues: shift, slow, quick quick slow, rock step, left foot
**ballroom-partner** (4 lessons): counting 1-2-3-4; order: welcome and what you will learn → where the dance comes from → the whole step at full speed, once → breakdown, one movement at a time → common mistakes and corrections; cues: side close side, step and point, turn turn turn, replace, side replace close
**latin-caribbean** (3 lessons): counting 1-3-5-7 with and-counts (and-1, and-3, and-5, and-7); order: welcome and what you will learn → where the dance comes from → the whole step at full speed, once → breakdown, one movement at a time → slow practice → adding the next layer (arms, turns, partner) → practice with the music, full tempo; cues: and one and three and five and seven, keep your feet together, relax your knees, bend the knees, change the weight
**classical-court** (3 lessons): counting 5-6-7-8; order: welcome and what you will learn → warm-up → where the dance comes from → breakdown, one movement at a time → common mistakes and corrections → practice with the music, full tempo; cues: keep length in both sides of the waist, keep the supporting leg strong and stable, keep the spine long, hold your placement, stay straight as a board
**stage-contemporary** (2 lessons): counting free-form / breath-based; order: welcome and what you will learn → warm-up → breakdown, one movement at a time; cues: deep slow hip circles, hips to one side in little pulses, breathe in arms come up, roll through the spine, belly rolls both directions
**ritual-sacred** (1 lesson): counting 1-2-3-4-5; order: welcome and what you will learn → the whole step at full speed, once → where the dance comes from → breakdown, one movement at a time → slow practice; cues: hold your knife out, every time the blade faces down, two thumbs are facing up

## Method

Each lesson is watched once by a vision-language model (first eight minutes, public YouTube URL, nothing downloaded) and reduced to a fixed form: structure with timestamps, counting, cues, demonstration, mistakes, safety, steps with counts, absorbability, verbatim quotes. The lists above are frequencies across all lessons; a line appears only when at least one teacher did it. Per-video records: `pedagogy/videos/`. Aggregate: `pedagogy/teaching_patterns.json`.
